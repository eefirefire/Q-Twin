Testing week Task 6 -- attention weight visualization
============================================================

Question: does an attention layer, trained with no knowledge of the
hand-derived early_displacement_30s biomarker, independently learn to
weight the early part of the probe curve most heavily?

Mean attention weight in the first 15s of the 45s window (seed 20260907): 0.299
(a uniform/no-preference attention would give 0.333 to that same span)
Peak mean attention weight occurs at t=38.1s

Multi-seed check (6 seeds, added after independent review found the
original version left this as an uncertain single-run claim): early-15s share per seed:
  seed=20260907: 0.299
  seed=1: 0.321
  seed=2: 0.307
  seed=3: 0.306
  seed=4: 0.315
  seed=5: 0.294
Mean across all 6 seeds: 0.307 (uniform=0.333). CONSISTENT direction across all seeds.

RESULT: NO -- attention does NOT clearly concentrate early; weight is spread
across the window (or concentrates elsewhere, e.g. near the end). This is
itself a genuinely interesting finding worth investigating further, not a
failed replication to hide: it suggests either (a) the model is picking up a
different, real signal than the hand-derived biomarker relies on, or (b) with
only 155 synthetic training curves, the attention layer hasn't converged to a
stable, interpretable pattern yet. See attention_weights_visualization.png
for the actual per-chip weight curves before drawing further conclusions.

VERIFIED ROBUST: checked across 6 seeds, not just the original single
run -- the direction (below uniform, i.e. NOT concentrating early) holds
consistently every time, upgrading this from a suggestive single observation
to a genuinely reproducible finding.
