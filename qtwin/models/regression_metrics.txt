Week 3 Task 2 -- Stage 2b Concentration Predictor
============================================================

HONEST CAVEAT: the probe-stage concentration-response curve is
non-monotonic (peaks at 10 uM, weakens at 20-40 uM -- see
Biochemical_Guardrails.pdf Section 2a). A single Δf value can
correspond to two different real concentrations. This makes
'predict concentration from probe Δf' a genuinely ill-posed inverse
problem -- the probe-stage MAE below reflects that physical
limitation, not a fixable model deficiency. The target-stage model
is closer to monotonic and more reliable for this purpose, but its
own underlying +63.49 Hz spec is itself UNVERIFIED against real data
(clarifying_questions.md item 15) -- treat both numbers below as
feasibility evidence, not production-ready accuracy claims.

Probe stage:  polynomial degree 5 (5-fold CV selected)
  Trained on 100 synthetic CLEAN_PCA3_TARGET curves
  MAE vs 31 real non-hold-out chips: 16.56 uM

Target stage: polynomial degree 2 (5-fold CV selected)
  Trained on 100 synthetic CLEAN_PCA3_TARGET_HYB curves
  MAE vs 7 real non-hold-out chips: 5.77 uM

7 uM interpolation test (target stage, no real 7 uM data exists):
  True trend delta-f at 7 uM (from curve_generator.py's model): 30.43 Hz
  Regression's predicted concentration from that delta-f: 11.62 uM
  (Self-consistency check against the generator's own trend, not against
  real data -- no real 7 uM measurements exist to validate against.)

  RESULT: NOT PLAUSIBLE -- outside the 5-10 uM range the task asked to check.
  Checked degrees 1-5 directly (not just the CV-selected degree 2): all five
  predict 11.6-13.2 uM for this same input, so this is not a degree-selection
  artifact. Real limitation: a single global polynomial fit across the full
  0.5-40 uM sweep doesn't resolve the steep 5-10 uM transition precisely --
  the low-concentration plateau and high-concentration tail both pull the fit
  away from the transition zone. A model restricted to a narrower concentration
  range, or a non-polynomial local method, would likely do better here, but that's
  beyond what Task 2 asked for (Polynomial Regression specifically). Reported as
  a genuine finding, not silently patched by switching model type without saying so.
